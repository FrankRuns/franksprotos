Making Million-Dollar Decisions.

One $30 million decision. Four activities. Two hours. No installations required.

← Presentation
Scenario: A national retailer serves 18 stores from a Chicago distribution center. Demand is shifting west. A new 200,000 sq. ft. DC would require an illustrative $30M upfront investment plus $4M in annual operating costs. You are the supply chain engineering team deciding whether to open another DC. All figures are illustrative synthetic classroom data, not real company data.

01 / Frame the problem · 30 min

Inspect the store demand and network map. What is the decision, who owns it, and how will you know if you've made a good one?

02 / Optimize, don't guess · 30 min

Choose which candidate sites the solver may consider, the maximum number of new DCs, and demand growth. Then compare a forced scenario with the best feasible network.

The in-browser enumerator searches all allowed site combinations (up to 3 new DCs), then allocates store demand under DC capacity constraints using min-cost flow. This is a small, exact discrete optimization demonstration, not a production MIP package. Each new DC costs $30M upfront, modeled as $3.06M/year annualized capital plus $4M/year operating. Distances are straight-line proxies.

Run a scenario to see results.

03 / AI as detective and skeptic · 30 min

Export your optimization result, then ask AI to explain and challenge it. Change one assumption and rerun the optimization.

Run the optimizer first, then generate a prompt.

Paste the prompt into your own AI chat. No API key is embedded or required. Treat AI claims as hypotheses and check them against the model.

04 / Tell a story that changes a decision · 30 min

The full hero's journey is useful, but you only have 60 seconds with an executive. Pick a short opening: insight, proof point, or lesson learned. These are classroom adaptations, not verbatim quotations from Mike Adams.

We thought our problem was ___, but the data revealed ___.

Volunteer pitch round: What is the decision? What makes the story persuasive? What evidence is missing?
